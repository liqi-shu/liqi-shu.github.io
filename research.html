Research

Completed work that moves from clinical uncertainty to usable evidence.

Our research sits at the intersection of vascular neurology, neurointervention, and data science. We use electronic health records, multicenter registries, imaging, and wearable sensors to answer practical questions in stroke prevention, treatment, and recovery.

Scope of this page: The projects below are completed and publicly available through peer-reviewed publications. Ongoing projects are intentionally not described.

Peri-procedural stroke: predicting risk before it becomes harm

Perioperative stroke is uncommon overall but disproportionately affects patients undergoing cardiac, major vascular, and neurosurgical procedures. Our work combines clinical synthesis with patient-level prediction.

In 2026, we published an EHR-derived model using 255,850 procedures for derivation and 189,095 procedures for external validation. The model used routine clinical and procedural information and achieved an external-validation AUC of 0.86, separating patients into clinically useful risk strata.

Clinical AI: turning free text into research-ready evidence

Stroke registries depend on labor-intensive chart review. In a completed multicenter study, we evaluated 22 open-source language models on 2,416 neurointerventional reports from patients treated for large-vessel-occlusion stroke.

The best model reached 94.8% overall extraction accuracy across eight clinically relevant variables and aligned more closely with expert interpretation than non-expert annotation for seven of eight variables. This work demonstrates a practical route to scalable, secure registry curation without custom preprocessing.

Cerebral venous thrombosis: treatment and long-term risk

Through international multicenter collaborations, we have studied anticoagulant treatment, recurrent thrombosis, and epilepsy after cerebral venous thrombosis (CVT).

The DIAS³ study developed a simple six-variable score and validated it in two independent cohorts. It estimates individualized 1- and 3-year epilepsy risk after CVT, supporting patient counseling and more personalized follow-up. Earlier ACTION-CVT work found that direct oral anticoagulants had comparable effectiveness to warfarin, with a lower risk of major hemorrhage in the primary multicenter analysis.

Cervical artery dissection: treatment, recurrence, and population trends

Completed nationwide and international studies have examined thrombolysis, recurrence, and changing incidence of cervical artery dissection.

Our 2025 nationwide analysis found that intravenous thrombolysis was associated with better odds of home discharge without a significant increase in intracerebral hemorrhage or in-hospital death. Separate work characterized US incidence trends and factors associated with recurrent stroke after dissection.

Neurorecovery: measuring what the affected arm actually does

Wrist accelerometers record movement, but conventional counts cannot reliably distinguish purposeful activity from nonfunctional motion. In completed work with stroke survivors, our best intrasubject machine-learning model reached 92.6% accuracy and closely tracked expert video annotation.

This line of work reflects a broader interest in pairing motion capture, imaging, and computational methods to make recovery measurement more sensitive to real-world function.
